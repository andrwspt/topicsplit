The problem: you read something great, but your notes are a mess

You finish a 3,000-word article. You want to save the key ideas into Obsidian, Logseq, or Notion. But the article does not have clean section breaks - it is one long wall of text.

So you either:

  • Copy-paste the whole thing into one giant note (useless - you will never find anything)
  • Manually highlight and split it into sections (takes 10-15 minutes per article)
  • Pay for an AI summarizer that ships your text to a server (and starts building a profile on you)

None of these are good. There is a better way.

The solution: split by meaning, not by word count

Semantic chunking vs word count splitting

Most text splitters work by character count or word count. They cut every N words regardless of meaning. This produces garbage - sentences get split mid-thought, and topics get scattered across multiple chunks.

TopicSplit uses lexical cohesion: it measures how many content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives) overlap between adjacent sentences. When overlap drops below a threshold, it marks a topic boundary. The result: segments that respect the natural structure of the text.

Word count splitting: "The cat sat on the mat. The dog..." becomes two chunks at word 10, splitting the sentence in half.

Semantic splitting (TopicSplit): "The cat sat on the mat. The dog barked at the mailman." stays together as one unit because both sentences share context about pets.
